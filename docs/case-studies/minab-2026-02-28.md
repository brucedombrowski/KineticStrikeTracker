# Case study: Minab school strike, 28 February 2026 — a null result

**Outcome: no instrumental detection.** The public seismic record contains nothing that can
be associated with this incident.

This is the project's most important validation case, precisely because the tool found
nothing. It is the worked example behind REQ-8.6 — *absence of a detection is not evidence
that no strike occurred* — and it demonstrates the failure mode the requirement exists to
prevent: a reader mistaking a silent report for a quiet day.

## The reported incident

| | |
|---|---|
| Date | 28 February 2026 |
| Location | Shajareh Tayyebeh girls' primary school, Minab, Hormozgan province, Iran |
| Coordinates used | 27.147 N, 57.080 E (town-level; the school's exact position was not established) |
| Reported | Struck three times; ~165–175 killed, predominantly schoolchildren |
| Status | Condemned by UN human rights experts and UNESCO. A US preliminary investigation reportedly attributed the strike to outdated targeting data. |

Sources are open reporting: OHCHR, UN News/UNESCO, Al Jazeera, Human Rights Watch, and US
congressional correspondence. Casualty figures are carried as reported, not derived
(REQ-11.5).

Note what could not be established: **the time of day.** That gap matters — it widens the
association window and disables the diurnal discriminant.

## What we did

Encoded the incident as a curated observation (REQ-2.18) in
[`data/seed/known-events.json`](../../data/seed/known-events.json), then queried the
instrumental record over the surrounding region and a five-month window:

```
kst ingest --seed data/seed/known-events.json --offline
kst ingest --start 2026-02-26T00:00:00Z --end 2026-07-25T23:59:59Z --bbox 24,28.5,53,59
kst report --bbox 24,28.5,53,59
```

Evidence retained: [`requirements/evidence/hormuz-2026-feb-jul.csv`](../../requirements/evidence/hormuz-2026-feb-jul.csv)
(SHA-256 `759de828…`).

## What the instrumental record contains

**54 observations across five months over the whole Hormuz region. Every one typed
`earthquake`. Not one typed `explosion`.**

On 28 February 2026, the entire region produced a single catalogued event:

| Time (UTC) | Agency | Type | Mag | Position | Uncertainty | Distance from Minab |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13:07:58 | IDC | earthquake | 3.7 mb | 26.815 N, 54.921 E | ±82 km | **~215 km** |

Nothing within 150 km of Minab on that date. The nearest event in the entire five-month
window is 84 km away, five weeks later, and typed as an earthquake.

## How the system reported it

The curated event stands alone. The correlation engine correctly declined to associate it
with the IDC event 215 km distant:

```
seed [C] minab-school-2026-02-28
  independent sources: 1 (instrumental 0)
```

That output is the correct behaviour and the whole point: a documented incident, entered as
ground truth, with **zero instrumental corroboration available**.

## Why nothing was detected

Applying the diagnostic order from [METHOD.md](../METHOD.md):

1. **No agency publishes explosion-typed events for Iran.** This is the dominant factor and
   it is editorial, not physical. Across five months and 54 events, every Iranian-region
   observation is typed `earthquake` — including events at operator-assigned depth 0.0.
   Israel's GSI publishes `explosion` types; Iranian and Gulf networks in this record do not.
2. **Magnitude.** Aircraft-delivered munitions couple at roughly M2.0–M2.5. Regional
   completeness here sits near M3.5 based on the smallest catalogued events in the window.
   The strike was likely below threshold for the networks that do report.
3. **Location precision would not have sufficed anyway.** The IDC origins in this region
   carry ±52–83 km uncertainty. Even a detection could not have been tied to a school.

## What this case establishes

- **A tool built only on public seismic catalogues would have been silent about one of the
  war's worst reported incidents.** Any report it produces must say so (REQ-8.6).
- **Coverage is agency-dependent, not region-dependent.** The same code over the same kind
  of event produces 4,800+ explosion-typed detections in the Levant and zero in Iran. The
  difference is who publishes, not what happened.
- **Site-level attribution is unreachable from public catalogues.** This is the strongest
  argument yet for independent triangulation (issue #3) and for non-seismic instrumental
  sources such as FIRMS thermal detections (REQ-2.15, issue #13), which do not depend on any
  agency's editorial choices.

## A caution about this case

Encoding an incident as a curated observation records **what was reported**. It does not
verify it, and the system must never present it as verified (REQ-11.4). The value of the
exercise is the comparison — reported event against instrumental record — and here the
comparison's honest result is *we cannot see it*, not *it did not happen*.
